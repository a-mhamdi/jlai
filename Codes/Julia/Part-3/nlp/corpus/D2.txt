Artificial intelligence combines sensing, computation, and decision-making to support diagnosis and treatment in modern medicine. Cardiac monitoring systems can use electrocardiogram sensors, blood pressure cuffs, or other sensors to perceive a patient's physiological state. The measurements are processed to estimate the heart's rhythm and understand signs of underlying disease.

Machine learning plays an important role in cardiac diagnosis. Machine learning models can recognize arrhythmias, classify risk levels, and help physicians interpret electrocardiogram observations. A system may use a neural network to detect irregular heartbeats or ischemia and then use a diagnostic algorithm to determine an appropriate course of treatment.

Modern healthcare increasingly uses embedded computers to perform these operations close to the patient. Processing data locally, such as on a wearable cardiac monitor, reduces the need to continuously send electrocardiogram readings or blood pressure measurements to a remote server. This is particularly important when a patient's condition can change quickly, such as during an episode of atrial fibrillation.

Cardiac monitoring devices can also communicate with other systems through wireless networks. For example, a group of connected monitors in a hospital could exchange information about a patient's heart rate, blood pressure, or alert status. This creates a system in which artificial intelligence, embedded computing, network communication, and cardiology work together to support safer, more responsive patient care.
