Autonomous robots combine sensing, computation, and decision-making to operate in environments without continuous human control. A mobile robot can use cameras, lidar, ultrasonic sensors, or other sensors to perceive its surroundings. The measurements are processed to estimate the robot's position and understand nearby objects. In cardiac surgery, robotic systems rely on similar sensing principles, using cameras and force sensors to guide instruments through the circulatory system and navigate around delicate structures like arteries and heart valves.

Artificial intelligence plays an important role in autonomous navigation. Machine learning models can recognize objects, classify environments, and help the robot interpret sensor observations. A robot may use a neural network to detect people or obstacles and then use a navigation algorithm to determine a safe path. During robot-assisted cardiac procedures, machine learning models can similarly interpret electrocardiogram signals and blood flow patterns in real time, helping the system respond to changes in heart rhythm or detect signs of ischemia during an operation.

Modern robots increasingly use embedded computers to perform these operations close to the sensors. Processing data locally reduces the need to continuously send camera images or sensor measurements to a remote server. This is particularly important when the robot must react quickly to a changing environment, such as a surgical robot compensating for the heart's continuous contraction and relaxation while operating on blood vessels.

Robots can also communicate with other devices through wireless networks. For example, a group of autonomous robots could exchange information about their position, detected obstacles, or mission status. In a hospital setting, a surgical robot can similarly transmit a patient's heart rate, blood pressure, and electrocardiogram data to a central monitoring station. This creates a system in which robotics, artificial intelligence, embedded computing, network communication, and cardiac monitoring work together to support safer, more responsive care.
