Electrical circuits rely on the flow of current through conductors, resistors, and capacitors. Ohm's law relates voltage, current, and resistance, while Kirchhoff's laws govern how current and voltage behave across a network of components. Engineers use transformers to step voltage up or down for efficient power transmission, and filters to isolate specific frequency ranges in a signal.

Modern power grids depend on careful load balancing between generation and demand, often coordinated through substations and control systems. Semiconductor devices such as diodes and transistors allow engineers to build amplifiers, switches, and logic gates, forming the foundation of both analog and digital electronics. As circuits grow more complex, simulation tools help predict how a design will behave before any physical prototype is built.
